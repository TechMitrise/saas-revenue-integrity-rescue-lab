## 1. Laravel/PHP SaaS rescue

**Trigger:** `[INCIDENT / JOB POST]` describes paying users, recurring failures or manual recovery inside an established Laravel/PHP application.

Your first risk is not framework age by itself. It is changing a revenue-critical path without knowing which state transitions, retries and repair steps currently protect customer access. I would start with a five-day Legacy SaaS Risk & Revenue Audit, map one payment-to-value path, reconstruct one representative failure and rank the controls that should change before broader modernisation.

The attached Rescue Lab is synthetic proof of the method: payment succeeds, fulfilment fails, evidence is correlated, access is reconciled once and a repeat is verified as harmless. The Audit deliverables remain useful whether I lead the later Stabilisation Sprint or your existing team implements them.

**Next step:** 20-minute fit call with the engineering owner and one real, sanitized failure example.

## 2. Stripe, billing, webhook or queue reliability

**Trigger:** `[INCIDENT / JOB POST]` mentions duplicate webhooks, delayed provisioning, failed jobs, missing upgrades or unsafe retries.

I would avoid beginning with another retry or webhook-handler patch. Those symptoms cross provider delivery, event identity, queue execution, database constraints and customer entitlement state. The proposed Legacy SaaS Risk & Revenue Audit traces one complete path and classifies findings as observed, correlated or hypothetical before recommending changes.

The Rescue Lab shows the exact evidence standard: ten signed deliveries become one event and one effect, three queue failures remain inspectable, and lock-protected reconciliation repairs access once before a repeat no-op. It demonstrates the investigation approach, not a claim about your system.

**Next step:** confirm the event type, customer value created and available correlation evidence.

## 3. Legacy SaaS takeover and modernisation

**Trigger:** `[ACQUISITION / HANDOVER / MODERNISATION JOB]` requires ownership of a revenue-producing PHP/MySQL system with limited documentation.

A takeover should separate business-critical stabilisation from broad rewrite decisions. I propose auditing one revenue path first: document the current state machine, identify hidden manual recovery and duplicate-effect risks, and produce a 30/60/90-day sequence with acceptance evidence.

The synthetic Rescue Lab demonstrates how I communicate that work to both founders and engineers: business impact first, correlation evidence second, constrained repair third, recurrence controls last. The initial Audit is fixed-scope at USD 1,500-3,000; implementation, modernisation and a reliability retainer are separate decisions after evidence.

**Next step:** select the highest-value payment-to-customer-value path for the audit.

The Rescue Lab gives you a safe technical proof to evaluate before introducing me: synthetic data only, explicit claim boundaries, reproducible tests and an inspectable recovery audit. I will not diagnose unrelated architecture during the fit call or use the audit to force a rewrite.
